# Desk Research — Round 1 Findings (2026-07-15)

Scope: hunt B2B "structural openings" (forced buyers via regulation) + "tolerated garbage" (niches stuck on spreadsheets). Goal = specific problems, not categories.

## Headline judgment
1. **Compliance-chasing is a trap right now.** The EU is *deregulating* for SMEs — the 18 Mar 2026 Omnibus cut CSRD to firms >1,000 employees AND >€450m revenue, and gave small suppliers a legal right to refuse data requests beyond the VSME scope. EUDR postponed to Dec 2026 / Jun 2027 with SME carve-outs. The "desperate small buyer with a deadline" keeps getting rescued by Brussels.
2. **Where hard deadlines remain (battery passport Feb 2027, Digital Product Passport), the space is already crowded** with funded startups selling to mid/large manufacturers via enterprise sales — a motion Måns can't run part-time. Avoid.
3. **The survivors are boring niche verticals on spreadsheets/WhatsApp/legacy tools.** Daily pain, no regulator about to erase it, reachable operators, cheap to acquire via distribution. Best fit for the edge and constraints.

## Candidates, scored against kill-criteria
Criteria: reachable buyer · real WTP · buildable+maintainable part-time · async · <$5k to validate · frequent/urgent pain.

### A. Niche vertical stuck on spreadsheets (pick ONE) — STRONGEST FIT
Examples surfaced: independent freight dispatch, construction logistics, commercial laundry, funeral homes, independent gyms/studios (member-retention analytics on top of booking tools). Pattern: high daily transaction volume, messy Excel + WhatsApp, owners who feel the pain.
- Fit: reachable ✓ · WTP ✓ (operational, daily) · buildable ✓ · async ✓ · <$5k ✓ · frequent ✓
- **Risk / unknowns:** these leads come from low-quality "boring niche" blogs — UNVERIFIED. Some have US incumbents. The real question is whether a *Nordic-language, localized* version is missing (the Cysel-style gap: incumbent exists in English/US, nothing good in Swedish).
- **Verdict:** highest fit, but only primary validation (talking to operators) proves the pain. This is where to spend the next two weeks.

### B. Small-supplier data-request fatigue (Nordic suppliers) — CONTESTED
Real, acute: >75% of large corps demand ESG/supplier data; EcoVadis (100k+), CDP (47k+) flooding small suppliers with overlapping questionnaires. Small firms without ESG teams drown.
- Fit: reachable ✓ · WTP moderate (it's cost-avoidance, not revenue) · buildable ✓ · async ✓ · <$5k ✓ · frequent ✓
- **Risk:** incumbents (Assent, Socialsuite, Greenproject) already here; the VSME standard is explicitly designed to *reduce* this pain from mid-2026. Betting against a regulator trying to kill your problem.
- **Verdict:** real but shrinking and contested. Backup, not lead.

### C. Battery Passport / Digital Product Passport data collection — POOR FIT
Hard deadline (18 Feb 2027 for batteries >2kWh), forced buyers, genuine data-collection pain across supply chains.
- Fit: buildable ✓ but reachable ✗ (mid/large manufacturers) · enterprise sales ✗ · capital-heavy ✗ · async ✗
- **Verdict:** real pain, wrong buyer for a part-time solo. Skip.

## What desk research CANNOT tell us
Whether operators in candidate A will actually *pay* and *switch*. That's not a search — it's 5–10 conversations. Desk research has done its job (killed compliance, pointed at boring verticals); the next move is talking to people.

## Recommended next step
Pick 2–3 spreadsheet-bound niches where Måns can reach owners warm (Nordic angle = language/localization moat). For each: find where they congregate, read their unprompted complaints, then get 5 on a call. Convert this list into a target list + interview script.
