# Desk Research — Round 2: The Solo Playbook + Niche Bets (2026-07-15)

Method this round: unconventional/primary sources — founder interviews, bootstrapper case studies, operator venting in forums/reviews. Reverse-engineer the winners, then scale the pattern down to what a no-phone solo can actually run.

## The model that fits Måns (this is the real output)
The vertical-SaaS giants (ServiceTitan, Squire, Toast) prove the *pain pattern* but their GTM — field sales + embedded payments — is the opposite of Måns's constraints. The right template is the **bootstrapped solo micro-SaaS**:
- **One painful workflow**, not a full platform. "People pay for tools that do one thing well."
- **Buyers outside the tech bubble with money** (accountants, contractors, clinics).
- **Distribution = long-tail SEO** ("best X software for [niche]") — Måns's proven edge, no phone.
- **Economics:** 70%+ margins, run for <$500/mo, $10k MRR typically 12–18 months in.
- **Proof:** PDF→Excel bank-statement converter for accountants = $16k MRR (one workflow). Arvid Kahl / FeedbackPanda = found the pain by watching ESL teachers vent in forums, co-built with them, SEO-led, later sold for life-changing money.

## The no-phone discovery engine
Find the exact pain by mining where operators complain (niche subreddits, Facebook groups, low-star G2/Capterra reviews of incumbents) and **scoring each complaint by frequency × emotional intensity**. This replaces early interviews. Output: the specific, repeated, angry sentence a buyer would pay to erase.

## Niche bets (evidence + honest risk), ranked
### A. Permit & inspection tracking for small contractors — STRONGEST
- **Evidence of real pain:** "every contractor has a permit horror story" — stale shared spreadsheets, 8 different AHJ portals with different logins, missed inspections, permits expiring unnoticed until the inspector shows up. Vivid, recurring, expensive.
- **Gap:** enterprise tools overpriced/overbuilt; spreadsheets underserve. Early small players (ExpiryEdge, PermitDesk) validate the wedge but haven't saturated it.
- **Fit:** self-serve ✓ · WTP ✓ (people already pay) · buildable part-time ✓ · SEO long-tail ✓ · frequent/urgent ✓
- **Risk:** US-centric ("AHJ" = US permitting); Nordic equivalent unproven. 2 early competitors already moving.

### B. Accounting / bookkeeping micro-workflows — STRONG
- Examples with proven revenue: bank-statement PDF→structured data ($16k MRR); dunning / failed-payment recovery (70–90% margins, revenue-linked).
- **Fit:** strong pain, clear WTP, self-serve.
- **Risk:** more horizontal than niche; competitive; less of an "underserved" story.

### C. Auto-repair shop management gaps — MEDIUM (backup)
- Incumbents (RepairShopr) feel dated; reporting shallow, inventory manual, billing errors slow cash flow at pickup.
- **Risk:** crowded; buyers expect a full suite, hard for a single-workflow wedge.

## Honest source-quality note
Most "boring niche" listicles hitting these searches are SEO content spam (ideaproof, bigideasdb, etc.) — discount them. The high-signal sources are founder interviews (YC, Contrary Research), Arvid Kahl's bootstrapper writing, Indie Hackers case posts, and *actual* review/forum complaints. Weight accordingly.
